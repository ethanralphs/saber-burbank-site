What to Know Before Your Kid's First Haircut

Published September 7, 2026 · 3 min read

The first haircut sounds simple enough until you're actually there, your two-year-old is squirming, and you realize you didn't think to bring a snack. It doesn't have to be stressful — but a little prep goes a long way. Here's what we see work, and what definitely doesn't, from the chairs at our shop on Victory Blvd.

Before you walk in

Timing matters more than most parents expect. Don't schedule a first haircut right before a nap or right after one — that groggy, disoriented window is rough on everyone. Mid-morning, when a kid is fed and awake, is usually the sweet spot. Bring something to hold their attention: a phone with a show they like, a small toy, anything that gives them a reason to sit still for ten minutes. We've seen parents pull out everything from dinosaur figurines to fruit snacks, and honestly, whatever works, works.

If your kid has never sat in a barber chair before, it helps to talk it up a little in the days before — not in an anxious "this is a big deal" way, just matter-of-fact. "We're going to get your hair cut Saturday, it's quick and it tickles a little." Kids who walk in knowing what to expect are almost always calmer than kids who had no idea where they were going.

Walk-ins are welcome at our shop, so there's no pressure to lock in a time — but if you know a Saturday morning works, you can also check in online before you leave the house to get in the queue. That way you're not standing around with a restless toddler.

What to tell the barber

You don't need a lot of hair vocabulary for a first cut. "Just a trim" or "clean it up" is a totally fine starting point. If there's a specific length or look you want, a photo on your phone is worth more than trying to describe it — just show us. For most little guys getting their first cut, the goal is usually to get the hair out of their eyes and off their ears, and that's easy to do in just a few minutes.

A couple of things that actually help the cut go smoothly: keep your own energy calm (kids read parents fast), and if they want to stay in your lap for the whole thing, that's fine. We've done plenty of cuts with a kid in Mom's or Dad's arms. Getting through it is more important than the perfect position.

After the cut, we offer an ear trim to clean up around the ears — worth asking about if the hair around the sides was getting long. For older boys who are used to the chair, a neck trim or even a massaging shampoo add-on can make the whole thing feel like a little treat rather than a chore.
